# TrueX sales and distribution SOP

Version: 1.0 · 22 October 2026

Owner: founder · Review: every Friday · First cycle: 30 calendar days

## 1. Mission and scope

Turn useful business deliverables into annual TrueX contracts and repeatable introductions to the next customer.

This SOP targets international business teams, with contracts priced in USD. The initial distribution hypothesis is fractional COOs and operations consultancies serving companies with 50–500 employees. The initial workflow is the weekly operating report: source updates become a cited summary, decisions, risks and actions.

The founder can change the segment or workflow at the weekly review. Change one variable at a time and record why. These are experiments, not validated market facts or revenue forecasts.

Run two acquisition experiments in the first cycle:

1. Operator introductions: help a trusted operator deliver a client workflow, then introduce TrueX to the client budget owner.
2. “The Worst Report on Earth”: rebuild a painful report in a public demonstration, using material explicitly cleared for publication.

Use the other plays in section 9 only when their entry conditions are met. This is a general business-team playbook; the separate SAP GTM draft remains a different proposal.

## 2. Owners, capacity and limits

| Role                   | Accountable for                                                                       | Default weekly allocation |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Founder                | Segment, recruitment, discovery, demonstrations, proposals, partner terms and closing | 15 hours                  |
| Growth operator        | Prospect research, scheduling, CRM, approved content and follow-up                    | 10 hours                  |
| Product/delivery owner | Demo readiness, output review, cost measurement and onboarding                        | 10 hours                  |

One person may hold multiple roles. If the founder is working alone, cap this program at 20 hours/week, prioritize operator introductions and reduce free proofs to three for the cycle. Keep the quality checks; reduce volume instead.

Default experiment limits, to be recorded before launch:

- Cash cap: $1,000 for acquisition experiments, excluding existing payroll, existing platform infrastructure and earned referral commissions. Include incremental model/tool charges, editing, prizes and any partner recruitment fees. Earned commissions are separately funded from collected customer revenue under the agreed partner terms and remain part of the acquisition-cost report. Record founder and team hours separately at an agreed internal hourly cost.
- Free proofs: six total, at most two delivery hours each. Initially allocate five to operator-led client workflows and one to a new challenge entry. Reuse the same accepted client output for the budget-owner review; extra challenge examples must reuse cleared work or synthetic inputs. A proof is one deliverable from one approved input set, with one correction round. Custom integration work requires a separate scope.
- Personalized public-data samples: three total, at most 45 minutes each, within the same cash cap.
- Work in progress: three proofs and five active proposals at once. Finish or explicitly defer work before starting more.
- Outreach: at most one initial message and two follow-ups per prospect in this cycle. Stop immediately on a refusal or opt-out.
- Founder records any change to these limits before spending or accepting extra work. This document does not itself spend money or authorize an agent to contact prospects.

## 3. Day-zero setup

The founder and delivery owner complete this before inviting anyone to a live demonstration:

- [ ] Assign an owner and backup for each role, or explicitly reduce capacity for a solo founder.
- [ ] Choose one workflow and one buyer: initially the operations leader who owns weekly reporting.
- [ ] Prepare a synthetic example, source files, baseline report and reviewed TrueX output.
- [ ] Run upload, generation, download and recovery from a failed request on the exact deployment being demonstrated.
- [ ] Record supported formats, input limits, enabled models, known limitations and the deployment owner. Check the [README](../../README.md) and [capability notes](../ASSISTANT_CAPABILITIES.md), then resolve conflicting or stale claims against the tested deployment.
- [ ] For private customer data, verify client access separation, approved model/tool data handling, storage location and a documented deletion process. A shared default signup workspace is not evidence of separate client workspaces.
- [ ] If those checks are incomplete, demonstrate only with public or synthetic material and leave private-data proofs pending.
- [ ] Create a CRM board, a suppression list for opt-outs, a calendar booking link and a controlled folder for approved evidence.
- [ ] Prepare a one-page offer and a proposal template with explicit scope, usage limits and supported capabilities.
- [ ] Create an intake form: contact, role, company, recurring workflow, next deadline, current effort, data sensitivity and permission to contact. Request files only through the approved intake route.

Do not promise certifications, SSO, private hosting, guest sharing or integrations unless the delivery owner verifies them for the offered deployment. Keep a dated capability sheet attached to each proposal.

## 4. The 30-day scoreboard

Targets are planning hypotheses. Procurement may continue beyond day 30; retain a dated next step rather than manufacturing a win or automatically declaring a loss.

| Metric                          | Cycle target | Count only when                                                                                |
| ------------------------------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Researched prospects            | 50           | 30 operators and 20 direct buyers, with a verified role, fit reason and source                 |
| Activated operators             | 5            | An operator has completed an agreed workflow with TrueX and committed to a client introduction |
| Qualified partner introductions | 15           | A unique client budget owner accepts an introduction and has a named recurring problem         |
| Accepted proofs                 | 5            | The workflow owner accepts the output against the written rubric                               |
| Paid annual customers           | 2            | The annual agreement is signed and the first contractual invoice is collected                  |
| Repeatable workflows            | 1            | The same workflow structure succeeds in two independent customer accounts                      |

For a solo founder, reduce the targets to three activated operators, nine qualified introductions, three accepted client proofs and one paid annual customer. Use a cleared existing proof or synthetic material for the challenge. The three-proof solo cap includes all channels.

Track the full funnel, including failures. Separate partner-sourced, challenge-sourced and direct prospects. Deduplicate a company appearing in several channels; record one primary source and any assisting sources. Content views and registrations are diagnostics, not substitutes for buyer progression.

## 5. Calendar and daily routine

| Period     | Founder                                                 | Growth/delivery owners                                              | Required output                                         |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Days 1–3   | Select workflow; approve offer; recruit first operators | Complete preflight; research the first 15 prospects; prepare sample | Tested demo, CRM and first prospect list                |
| Days 4–7   | Run operator discovery calls                            | Finish the 50-name research list; schedule the challenge            | First proofs booked; challenge date and intake ready    |
| Days 8–14  | Co-deliver proofs; host the challenge                   | Review entries, record measurements and prepare approved clips      | Operator results and one public demonstration           |
| Days 15–21 | Meet introduced budget owners; present proposals        | Deliver remaining proofs; record security and procurement steps     | Qualified opportunities with dated next actions         |
| Days 22–30 | Resolve objections; close and onboard                   | Collect invoices; track activation; package reusable workflow       | Paid contracts or documented buying plans; cycle review |

Every business day:

1. Spend 15 minutes reviewing due next actions, opt-outs, deadlines and delivery capacity.
2. Research and send up to five individually relevant first messages while the 50-name pool remains uncontacted. Use an appropriate permitted channel; verify the personalization and any sample before sending.
3. Complete due follow-ups and run booked calls. Use day 3 and day 8 after the initial message as defaults; a reply replaces that schedule with an agreed next step.
4. Work on the highest-priority proof or proposal within the capacity cap.
5. Spend 15 minutes logging responses, time, costs, stage changes and tomorrow's next actions.

Every Friday, run a 45-minute review: inspect the funnel by source, listen to objections, review one failed proof, check spend and choose one change for next week. Record the decision and its owner.

## 6. Play A: recruit operators who can introduce buyers

### A1. Find a credible operator

Use public professional profiles, consultancy websites, relevant communities and existing introductions. Select someone who serves multiple relevant clients, owns an actual reporting process and can describe who approves spending. Record the evidence; follower count alone does not qualify them.

### A2. Send a specific invitation

> Hi [name] — I saw your work on [verified client problem or published example]. I'm building TrueX around a specific job: turning weekly updates into a usable operating report. I'd like to rebuild one sanitized example with you in a short working session. If it genuinely helps your client delivery, we can discuss bringing it to one client team. Worth a 20-minute conversation?

First follow-up:

> One concrete output we can test is [relevant deliverable]. We'd measure preparation time, corrections and whether the owner would actually send it. Is that a recurring problem in your client work?

Final follow-up:

> I'll close this out for now. If [workflow] becomes a priority, happy to pick it up with one small example.

Templates are drafts for a human to personalize and send. Do not invent a relationship, client, result or claim.

### A3. Run a 20-minute discovery call

Ask these questions and record the answers:

1. Which recurring deliverable consumes time every week, and who uses it?
2. How is it made today, including checking and corrections?
3. Who owns the budget and what event makes improvement urgent?
4. What data can be used, and what security or procurement constraints apply?
5. If the output passes an agreed test, will you introduce us to that budget owner?

Advance only with a real workflow owner, permitted inputs, an acceptance rubric and a scheduled review. If the operator cannot introduce a buyer, classify them as a research or content contact rather than a distribution partner.

### A4. Deliver one proof

Use section 8. Have the operator review the result before showing it to the client. Log the operator's contribution separately from TrueX and founder work so the experiment does not mistake unpaid consulting for product performance.

### A5. Agree the partner offer

Starting offer to test: the operator can charge the customer separately for their own implementation services, with scope and responsibilities disclosed. TrueX can offer 20% of first-year collected platform subscription revenue for an eligible referred customer.

Before promising a commission, the founder records an agreed written arrangement covering:

- Referral registration and acceptance before the opportunity is already active in TrueX's pipeline; a 90-day attribution window, extendable in writing for an active procurement process.
- One commission recipient per account; disputes resolved before quoting a payout.
- Commission excludes taxes, refunds, AI usage and onboarding fees. Refunds reverse the corresponding commission; payout follows collection and the agreed refund period.
- The 20% applies only to the first contract year. Renewal incentives require a separate agreement.
- The customer knows about the commercial relationship. The partner cannot promise product features, sign customer contracts for TrueX or publish customer material without permission.
- The founder checks the remaining margin, including support, before accepting the deal.

The rate and terms are hypotheses, not an existing partner program or a commitment made by this SOP.

### A6. Ask for a named introduction

> We agreed that [deliverable] is usable and measured [actual result, including review time]. Who owns this process and budget at [client]? Could you introduce us for a 25-minute working review? We'll show the result, discuss their requirements and decide whether an annual workspace makes sense.

One successful proof should lead to one specific introduction first. Expand to the operator's other clients after the first introduction produces a qualified buying conversation.

## 7. Play B: “The Worst Report on Earth”

### B1. Open a narrowly defined challenge

Publish through the founder's existing accounts, participating operators and communities where the organizer permits it:

> Bring the weekly report your team hates making. We'll select up to three sanitized examples and rebuild them live in TrueX. We will show the time, the corrections and the final result. Submit the workflow and a cleared sample by [date]. Selection is based on fit and our delivery capacity.

Limit any promised prize to the recorded budget. Do not offer an uncapped free annual service or imply a winner has already achieved an unmeasured saving.

### B2. Separate intake from publication permission

The growth operator checks the contributor's authority to share the material. The delivery owner screens it for confidential or personal information. Use synthetic material if authority or sensitivity is uncertain.

Permission to assess an entry does not grant permission to publish it. Obtain separate written permission identifying the material, company/name attribution and publication channels. A refusal does not block a private sales conversation. Do not accept private files through public comments.

### B3. Select and rehearse

Choose up to three examples based on recurring pain, relevant buyer access and fit with the same reporting workflow. The default capacity allows only one new custom challenge proof; use already cleared work or synthetic inputs for additional examples. If fewer qualify, use fewer. Rehearse each on the tested deployment, measure baseline and review effort, and keep a labeled fallback recording.

### B4. Run a 30-minute live session

1. Minutes 0–5: explain the task, permitted inputs and scoring rubric.
2. Minutes 5–20: show one primary transformation with source checks and review. Summarize additional examples only if time allows.
3. Minutes 20–25: ask the owner to score usefulness and corrections. Separate observed results from estimates.
4. Minutes 25–30: invite relevant budget owners to a private workflow review.

If generation fails, show the failure, switch to the clearly labeled rehearsal and book a private rerun. Never present a recording as live. A material factual error is corrected before publication of the final artifact.

### B5. Convert interest within two business days

> You mentioned [specific workflow] during the session. We can review one approved example with its owner and the person responsible for budget. What is your next reporting deadline, and who should join?

Publish only approved material. Create one before/after clip, one factual breakdown and one invitation from the same session. Link to the same intake route and record the source. Close or delete rejected input files within seven days after the selection decision unless a different retention period was agreed; the owner records completion.

## 8. Shared proof and demonstration procedure

### Entry checklist

- [ ] Workflow owner, budget-owner path and decision date recorded.
- [ ] One deliverable, one input set, baseline and correction allowance agreed.
- [ ] Data authorization and deployment checks complete.
- [ ] Free-proof capacity and cash limits available.
- [ ] Review meeting booked before work starts.

### Execute and measure

1. Capture current preparation and review time from an observed comparable task where possible. Mark self-reported or estimated baselines explicitly.
2. Produce the artifact. Record generation time, human preparation, checking, corrections, direct model/tool cost and any failures.
3. Verify factual claims against the supplied sources. Do not fill missing business facts with guesses.
4. Ask the workflow owner to review the output using this rubric: required sections complete; figures and material claims supported; actions and owners represented correctly; no critical errors; usable within the agreed correction allowance.
5. Record accepted, accepted after correction, rejected or blocked. Record the reason and the owner's written decision.
6. If accepted, book the budget-owner/proposal meeting. If rejected, allow the agreed correction round or stop and explain the limitation. Never expand the free scope silently.

Default improvement hypothesis: 30% less total human preparation and review time at the agreed quality. Replace it with a buyer-specific threshold before starting. With an unknown baseline, report acceptance and observed effort; do not claim a percentage improvement.

Human time saved = baseline human effort − new human preparation, checking and correction effort. Track elapsed turnaround separately. Time released is capacity value; cash savings require evidence of a real expenditure reduction.

## 9. Four additional plays: launch conditions and procedure

### C. Apply with finished work

Entry: the delivery owner has one reliable public-data workflow and capacity remains for a personalized sample.

1. Pick a qualified account and a visible business event.
2. Create a short, cited briefing using public company material. State what private information is unavailable.
3. Check every company-specific claim and record creation time.
4. Send a private two-minute walkthrough with one invitation to review the recurring version.
5. Log the buyer response and source. Do not imply access to internal data or endorsement by the company.

Script: “I built a short [deliverable] from your published [sources] for [business event]. These are public-data findings, and these sections need your team's context. Is this useful enough to review with the person who owns the recurring process?”

### D. Board Meeting Rescue

Entry: a qualified buyer has a real meeting deadline and approved inputs; delivery capacity is confirmed.

1. Name one deliverable, acceptance owner and deadline.
2. Confirm that the exact deployment can handle the material and agree a fallback.
3. Scope any paid one-off work separately from the annual subscription. Obtain agreement before delivery begins.
4. Deliver and review before the customer's deadline, leaving an agreed human review window.
5. Discuss an annual contract only if the workflow recurs. Record rescue revenue separately from subscription ARR.

Script: “For your [date] review, we can help prepare [specific output] from [approved inputs], subject to the agreed checks. If the result works and this repeats, we can put the recurring process into an annual workspace.”

### E. Give the internal champion evidence

Entry: a workflow owner has an accepted proof and wants to take it to a manager.

1. Prepare a one-page internal business case: baseline, measured effort, quality, proposed annual spend, implementation effort and limitations.
2. Credit the champion's contribution accurately.
3. Ask them to invite their manager or budget owner to a decision meeting.
4. Keep the evidence private unless separately cleared for publication.
5. Record the next department introduction only after that department agrees to a conversation.

Script: “Here is the measured result and the work you contributed. Which manager owns the recurring process and budget? Let's review the business case with them.”

### F. Expert workflow drops and Workflow Deathmatch

Entry: two independent accounts have accepted the same workflow structure, and at least one reusable result is cleared for publication. Run one format per cycle.

For an expert drop: choose an operator with a relevant audience, co-create one named workflow and sample, agree attribution and any payment, publish through an approved channel and route interested buyers to discovery. Describe manual setup honestly; do not advertise one-click template installation unless verified.

For a deathmatch: define the same task and inputs, disclose each approach's configuration and reviewer role, cap effort equally, and score accuracy, total review time, cost and usability. Publish weaknesses and losses alongside wins. Obtain permission for submitted material; comparisons support only what was actually tested.

Measure qualified buying conversations per hour and dollar spent. Audience size alone does not justify repeating either format.

## 10. Convert a result into an annual contract

### Qualify the buying process

The founder confirms the budget owner, annual budget range, decision criteria, procurement/security owners, required capabilities and a realistic signature date. A happy user without a budget path stays in discovery.

### Present one concrete offer

Starting price hypothesis from this planning session:

- $18,000/year workspace, up to 50 named users, billed annually upfront.
- $3,000 one-time onboarding for one scoped workflow.
- Separately prepaid AI usage, initially discuss a $300 budget, with clear rates and customer-approved increases.
- Storage, support, implementation hours and any overage rules must be explicitly stated. Custom integrations and dedicated deployment need separate scope and feasibility review.

The price is a test, not a published commitment. The founder checks delivery cost, partner commission and capacity before quoting. Confirm billing and budget enforcement exist on the offered deployment; if they do not, define and verify a bounded manual operating process before paid usage begins.

Proposal script:

> We tested [workflow] with [owner] and observed [actual evidence]. The recurring scope is [deliverable and cadence]. The annual workspace is [price], onboarding is [scope and price], and AI usage is [budget and rates]. These capabilities are available; these requirements remain unresolved. Who needs to approve this, and what is the next dated step?

### Contract and handoff checklist

- [ ] Scope, named buyer, delivery responsibilities and acceptance criteria are recorded.
- [ ] Security, data handling, support, term, renewal, cancellation and credit treatment are agreed through the customer's buying process.
- [ ] Unsupported requirements are resolved or explicitly excluded in the offer.
- [ ] Partner attribution and margin are checked before signature.
- [ ] Agreement signed; invoice issued; collection owner and due date recorded.
- [ ] Mark “paid annual customer” only after the first contractual payment clears. Track signed/unpaid separately.
- [ ] Delivery owner accepts handoff and books kickoff with the customer.
- [ ] Target first accepted recurring deliverable within five business days of receiving access and approved inputs; log dependencies and escalate a missed date.
- [ ] Request another internal introduction after demonstrated value. Ask for a public reference separately.

## 11. CRM and measurement

Use one row per account and a linked activity log. Keep private source documents in the controlled delivery workspace, not in a broadly shared CRM.

Required account fields: account ID; company; buyer and role; primary source; assisting sources; partner ID if applicable; public source/fit evidence; workflow and deadline; stage; owner; next action and date; contact permission/opt-out status; proof status and evidence link; security/procurement blocker; proposed platform ACV; setup fee; usage budget; commission; contract status; invoice/collection status; delivery hours/cost; loss reason.

Stages and required evidence:

| Stage                 | Evidence required for stage                                                                      | Owner              |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Researched            | Fit reason and appropriate contact route verified                                                | Growth             |
| Contacted             | Initial message sent and next action scheduled                                                   | Growth             |
| Discovery             | Problem, workflow owner and buying path recorded                                                 | Founder            |
| Qualified             | Buyer path, approved proof inputs, rubric and review meeting confirmed                           | Founder            |
| Proof                 | Owner, rubric and review booked; record acceptance, rejection or blocked reason before advancing | Delivery           |
| Proposal              | Scope and price reviewed with buyer; dated decision plan                                         | Founder            |
| Procurement           | Required decision-makers and unresolved requirements identified                                  | Founder            |
| Signed/unpaid         | Signed agreement and invoice with collection date                                                | Founder            |
| Paid/onboarding       | First contractual payment received; kickoff accepted by delivery                                 | Founder + delivery |
| Active                | First recurring output accepted; next delivery scheduled                                         | Delivery           |
| Nurture / closed lost | Explicit reason and follow-up date, or a decision to stop                                        | Founder            |

Report weekly:

- Unique accounts contacted; replies; qualified buying conversations, all by source.
- Accepted proofs / completed proofs, with blocked proofs listed separately.
- For the cohort of accepted-proof accounts whose agreed decision date has passed: paid annual customers in that cohort / all accounts in that cohort. Also report still-open and not-yet-due accounts separately; do not hide them by changing the denominator.
- Collected platform revenue, setup fees and consumed usage separately. Count committed annual platform subscriptions in platform ARR; exclude one-time services and uncommitted usage.
- Acquisition cost: attributable acquisition cash spend excluding payroll and commissions + costed sales/content/proof labor + earned partner commission, divided by paid new customers. Count each expense once. If none have paid, report spend and hours; the cost per acquired customer is not yet established.
- Delivery effort per output; factual error/correction rate; first-value time; contract-to-collection days.
- Introductions and qualified opportunities per activated operator. Track follow-through rather than counting partner agreements alone.

## 12. Continue, adjust or stop

These thresholds are initial operating rules, not statistical proof. The founder reviews context and records any override.

| Signal                                                                                       | Required response                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fewer than three substantive replies after 15 relevant first messages and due follow-ups     | Inspect targeting and the invitation; change one before using the rest of the list                   |
| Three completed operator proofs produce zero accepted client introductions                   | Stop free operator delivery; determine whether the problem is incentives, buyer access or weak value |
| Three similar proofs need more than the two-hour cap                                         | Narrow the deliverable, price the service or change the workflow; do not call it repeatable yet      |
| A material error makes a proof unsafe or unusable                                            | Pause use of that artifact; correct it, notify its owner and retest before reusing it                |
| Three accepted proofs reach a buying decision and none advances to a proposal or procurement | Review urgency, budget access and offer; do not respond by increasing content volume                 |
| Challenge creates attention but no qualified buying conversations within seven days          | Complete the follow-up review, then pause event production and inspect audience and CTA              |
| Spend or work-in-progress cap is reached                                                     | Stop new commitments; finish active work or explicitly reset limits                                  |
| Buyers require unverified security or deployment capabilities                                | Put the opportunity in a blocked stage; scope feasibility before making a promise                    |

Expand a channel's budget only after it produces two paid annual accounts, at least one completed onboarding and an acquisition/delivery cost review. Small counts justify another controlled experiment, not a claim that the channel scales.

At day 30, write a one-page cycle review: source funnel, buyer objections, paid and pending deals, costed effort, workflow reuse, delivery failures, partner follow-through and one decision for the next cycle. Keep the strongest channel and one supporting experiment.
